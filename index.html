i. The Land

A working farm in the
quiet end of the island.

Menemsha Farms sits on seven acres in Chilmark, where the western light comes long and slow over the pond.

Joe and his family have stewarded the land since 1999, in the old way — orchard rows kept by hand, bees moved with the season, hens that know their keepers by step. Nothing is rushed here. The peaches ripen when peaches ripen.

The farm is small on purpose. It is what one family can hold well, and what the land can give without complaint. The work is quiet, mostly, and constant, and beautiful.

— A note from the family

Stewardship

Three generations on the same seven acres, with a fourth on the way.

Practice

Low-spray orchard care, pasture-raised hens, and bees that work the wild meadows beside the rows.

Place

Chilmark, Martha's Vineyard. Five minutes from the harbor, a world away from everything else.

iv. A Note on Color

The colors of this place
were matched by hand.

The two colors that mark our farm — and now this site — were not chosen from a catalogue. Joe pulled them straight from the property. The crimson came from a berry on a bush by the house. The green came from a piece of moss off a tree on the land. Both were carried to the paint shop and matched by eye.

Berry Crimson

Matched from · Berry bush by the house

A soft, slightly muted red. The color of a single berry held up to the light, not the color of the bush from across the yard.

#BC4853

Tree Moss

Matched from · Moss on the old oak

A quiet, dusty green. The color you only really see when you bring a piece of bark inside and set it on the kitchen table.
